# Meno — presenter notes for tonight

Use alongside [the action-by-action runbook](DEMO_RUNBOOK.md). The learner persona and opportunity briefs are fictional and must be labeled **DEMO DATA**. The app's saved state changes are real prototype actions.

## Opening — about 30 seconds

“Meno helps people who have already started learning tech but become stuck or lose direction. Learn anywhere. Get mentored here. Our returning JavaScript learner has a course and some retained knowledge. We help them find one useful next step, keep their progress, and understand how it connects to an opportunity direction.”

## What to say while the state changes

- **Course:** “We bring their existing course into the mentor's context. Confirming an outline does not prove a skill, and we keep their self-reported place.”
- **Diagnostic:** “These are actual answers to fixed questions. Functions and arrays can show retained knowledge while promises need review. This observation is narrow; it is not a certification.”
- **Next step and mentor:** “The suggestion has an evidence basis and a source. The learner chooses whether to accept it. The AI explanation uses their saved context; chat alone cannot change assessed evidence.”
- **Practical work:** “We save and submit guided choices, then explicitly record scoped feedback. This shows what happened in this scaffold. It does not claim that the learner independently built and ran an app.”
- **Opportunity comparison:** “This is an example, not a live vacancy. We saved the baseline before the work. Guided preparation has changed; independent project proof and Git evidence are still needed. There is no readiness score that blocks the learner.”
- **Recovery:** “We deliberately simulate a pause. Meno asks rather than diagnoses. The five-minute proposal stays pending while their existing step and evidence remain. Accepting changes the plan; declining keeps it.”

## Closing — about 20 seconds

“The result is a useful next move with retained history and an honest connection to an opportunity direction. Our prototype demonstrates that loop. Sustained retention, independently assessed project quality and employment outcomes still need learner research and longer-term evaluation.”

## Questions to answer directly

| Question | Accurate answer |
|---|---|
| Is this another course platform? | It supplements the courses, classes and self-study a learner already uses. |
| What is AI doing? | Reviewed outline suggestions and course-aware mentor explanations. Authorized database functions control evidence and plan changes. |
| Are the jobs real? | Tonight's three briefs are visibly fictional examples; they have no application links or claimed employer eligibility. |
| Did completing the scaffold prove job readiness? | It recorded guided preparation within a fixed rubric. Independent artifact evidence remains a separate gap. |
| Are reminders or missed-session detection running? | No. The check-in is learner-initiated; the pause in the presentation is deliberately simulated. |
| What happens if AI is unavailable? | Saved course, diagnostic, proposals, guided work, feedback and opportunity comparisons remain usable. The mentor shows its failure honestly. |
| What does the hackathon prove? | A persistent, inspectable learning-to-opportunity loop with actual state changes. It does not yet establish long-term learning, retention or hiring outcomes. |

Keep credentials and private learner details out of the presentation. Use a disclosed prior recording only as a connection/provider fallback; never present it as a live response.
